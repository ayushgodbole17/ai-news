01  ·  speech  ·  arXiv
IndicFDB: Benchmarking Full-Duplex Voice Agents across Indian Languages

IndicFDB is a new benchmark for full-duplex voice agents across Indian languages, built because the existing Full-Duplex-Bench is English-only and relies on word-timestamped ASR plus an English-prompted LLM judge.

Why you care: A direct hit on their exact problem space: evaluating full-duplex voice-agent turn-taking and barge-in quality for Indian languages.

02  ·  eval  ·  arXiv
mu-bench: A Multilingual Utterance Transcription Benchmark

mu-bench is a dataset of 4,270 call-center-style utterances built to evaluate ASR for voice agents on semantic correctness rather than the surface-level word-error-rate metric typically used on read, English-centric speech.

Why you care: A more realistic ASR evaluation methodology than raw WER, directly applicable to grading voice-agent transcription quality on real calls.

03  ·  eval  ·  arXiv
CoSE-E: A Benchmark for Code-switched Speech Evaluation in Enterprise Settings

CoSE-E is a benchmark for evaluating code-switched speech recognition specifically in enterprise settings, going beyond edit-distance metrics to measure operational impact.

Why you care: Code-mixed speech is called out as a constant problem in their work, and this benchmark targets exactly that in an enterprise/call context.

04  ·  speech  ·  arXiv
How to Reduce Whisper Hallucination

An engineering writeup on Whisper's hallucination problem: on 42 clips of pure room tone, whisper-large-v3 emits words on 61.9% of them and emits something on 100% of them.

Why you care: Whisper-family ASR hallucinating on silence or noise is a direct production issue for any voice-agent pipeline using it.

05  ·  speech  ·  arXiv
Whisper-Flash: Acoustically Conditioned Parallel Drafting for Faster Whisper Decoding

Whisper-Flash applies acoustically-conditioned parallel drafting, a form of speculative decoding, to speed up Whisper's token-by-token decoder, which normally dominates inference time since the encoder reads the whole utterance in one parallel pass.

Why you care: A concrete latency-reduction technique for ASR decoding, relevant to their per-call latency budgets.
