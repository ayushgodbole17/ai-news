I'm an AI engineer at a startup, and my goal is to be the most informed, up-to-date AI
engineer I can be. My projects change week to week, so I want the whole picture, not a
feed tuned to one stack or product. Three things matter equally:

1. Useful AI: the important new models, tools, APIs and research, with real substance
   (what it does, benchmarks, pricing, limits). I want genuinely new kinds of things the
   first time they appear, so I hear about them early. Follow-ups, clones and look-alikes
   of something already covered are not news unless they change something big.
2. Engineering: how strong teams build and run systems. Backend architecture, databases,
   distributed systems, reliability, observability, security, postmortems and migration
   stories with real lessons.
3. AI engineering, where the two meet: putting models into production. Serving and
   inference cost and latency, evals and LLM-as-judge, RAG, agents that actually work,
   guardrails and AI governance, and the tools that make building with AI better
   (including coding agents, when something is notable).

My recent work, for context only (it changes): AI governance and guardrails, backend
engineering (Python/FastAPI, Node/TypeScript, MongoDB, Docker), real-time voice and
speech AI, and Gemini-heavy LLM pipelines.

The industry side matters too: funding, executive moves, policy and regulation, and
commentary worth reading.

I do not care about: routine version bumps, outage chatter, listicles and "top N tools"
roundups, vendor case studies, or hype without substance.
